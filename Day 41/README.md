🧠 Day 41 — Understanding the Evolution from RNNs to LLMs

Continuing my 100 Days of Deep Learning journey, today I started exploring the evolution that eventually led to Large Language Models (LLMs).

Instead of looking at LLMs as a black box, I focused on understanding the major stages that came before them:

Encoder–Decoder → Attention → Transformers → Transfer Learning → LLMs

📚 What I learned
🔹 Stage 1 — Encoder–Decoder: Understanding how LSTM-based encoder and decoder architectures were used for sequence-to-sequence tasks.
🔹 Stage 2 — Attention: Moving beyond relying only on the encoder's final state by allowing the decoder to focus on the relevant parts of the input.
🔹 Stage 3 — Transformers: Understanding the transition from recurrent architectures toward Transformer-based architectures using attention.
🔹 Stage 4 — Transfer Learning: Learning how pretrained models can be reused and adapted for different deep learning tasks.
🔹 Stage 5 — LLMs: Understanding the major requirements behind large language models — massive and diverse data, GPU clusters, extensive training, and significant computational infrastructure.
🔹 RLHF: Introduction to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback as part of the LLM development pipeline.

One important takeaway from today's learning:

LLMs didn't appear overnight. They are the result of multiple developments in sequence modeling, attention, Transformers, and transfer learning.

This is where my Deep Learning → NLP → Transformers → LLMs journey starts becoming much more connected.
