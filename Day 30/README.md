Day 30 — Backpropagation in CNNs 🧠🔄

Continuing my #100DaysOfDeepLearning journey, today I went deeper into one of the most important concepts behind training neural networks: Backpropagation in CNNs.

Today I worked through the complete flow of how a CNN learns from its error:

Forward propagation through convolution, ReLU, max pooling, flattening, and fully connected layers
Identifying trainable parameters — weights and biases
Binary Cross-Entropy as the loss function
Using Gradient Descent to update weights and biases
Applying the Chain Rule to calculate gradients
Deriving gradients for the fully connected layer
Understanding the important result for Sigmoid + Binary Cross-Entropy
Backpropagating gradients through the Flatten layer
Understanding how gradients flow backward through the network to minimize the loss

The key takeaway today was understanding that backpropagation is essentially the chain rule applied backward through the computational graph to calculate the gradients needed for gradient descent.

What made today's learning interesting was connecting the mathematics with the actual CNN pipeline instead of treating backpropagation as just a formula.
